Abstract

Recent video generation models provide a scalable source of synthesis data for robotic manipulation, which is particularly valuable for humanoid robots whose long-horizon demonstrations are costly to collect. However, visual plausibility alone is insufficient for robot learning: a locally inconsistent interaction can invalidate the task state and corrupt all subsequent actions. We identify accumulated transition errors, rather than generation capacity alone, as a key bottleneck in long-horizon robotic video generation. We present SAVIOR (State-Guided Augmented Verification for Iterative Observation Rollouts), a closed-loop framework that reformulates video generation as the recursive evolution of a verified task state. Instead of autoregressively propagating every generated transition, SAVIOR maintains explicit awareness of task progress, validates whether each transition advances the manipulation objective while preserving previously verified states, and regenerates failed segments before they corrupt future rollout. Across open-source and commercial video generators, SAVIOR consistently improves long-horizon, spatial, and semantic manipulation tasks while enabling more reliable action recovery and humanoid execution. Our analysis further reveals that many rollout failures are local and recoverable, underscoring the importance of verified task progression for scalable generation of executable manipulation experience.

Key insight

The bottleneck in long-horizon robotic video generation is not synthesis capacity, but accumulated transition error. Once each step is gated by verified task progress, recovery stops being an afterthought— it emerges as strategy change, tool selection, and instruction refinement, turning locally invalid motions into recoverable control events rather than irreversible rollout collapse.
